## Proposed contribution and specific question

Perception and embodied evaluation of spatial permission cues

Proposed fit: Perception and embodied evaluation of spatial permission cues. This is an interpretation of the published work, not an attributed position or agreed collaboration.

**Spatial case (proposal):** I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture PhD proposal on AI agents and robots representing absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would compare an ordinary library threshold with a mixed-reality version that displays where a representative’s permission ends. Participants could cancel the instruction and continue personally. The study would separate perceived distance, route choice and comprehension of authority from the actual geometry, so an apparently convenient representation does not obscure a detour or human handoff.

**First ask:** Could we discuss how to measure these perceptual effects and define an interpretable, reversible cue suitable for an architectural doctoral experiment?
