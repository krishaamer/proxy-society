Dear Architect Kim,

In your practice’s interview on terrace housing units, you distinguish privacy from opportunities for chance encounters (https://samhyundosi.com/2021/11/09/%EC%9C%A0%EB%8B%9B%EC%9C%BC%EB%A1%9C-%EC%8C%93%EC%95%84%EC%98%AC%EB%A6%B0-%EA%B0%80%EB%8A%A5%EC%84%B1%EC%9D%98-%EC%84%B8%EA%B3%84/).

For Proxy Society, I am exploring what happens when an AI collects or books something for an absent person. One proposed comparison places a shared service counter directly on a residents’ terrace route; another keeps a small screened handoff recess beside it. I would examine whether convenience exposes residents or leaves them a choice about encountering others.

Which boundary would you change first to preserve privacy while keeping chance meetings possible? A short critique of a plan and section, or a relevant Busan housing/common-space precedent, would help sharpen the PhD question.
