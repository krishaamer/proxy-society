## Prepared text

Dear Professor Hennig,

The Highland Game makes participants’ preferred land uses and responses to others’ ideas visible through a shared map (https://geovis.hi.is/research/highland-game-iceland/). Its participatory format suggests a useful test for delegated representation.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. In a proposed civic-room study, one layout would place a map and visible mandate log beside a staffed table; another would separate automated submissions from that table. Residents could contribute personally or authorize an AI proxy, then inspect and revoke its contribution.

Could you advise how the mapping should distinguish an authorized contribution from inferred preferences, and whether this question fits your Geography doctoral research and current supervisory capacity?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
