## Prepared text

Dear Professor Dainese,

Your Architecture and Care course treats design as a responsibility involving social justice, welfare and active civic participation (https://ugla.lhi.is/kennsluskra/index.php?chapter=namskeid&id=77334920266&tab=nam). I would like to test care through the arrangement of assistance, rather than assume delegation improves it.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. In a proposed community-building study, a staffed care point would be placed either beside the ordinary entrance or behind a proxy queue. A staged AI representative could perform a bounded task, with refusal and revocation visible; personal participation would remain available. Interviews would examine whose judgement defines successful assistance.

Could you critique the care assumptions in this spatial comparison, especially how to avoid replacing people’s accounts of need, and suggest an appropriate architectural research or doctoral connection?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
