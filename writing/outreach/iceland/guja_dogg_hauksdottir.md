## Prepared text

Dear Professor Hauksdóttir,

Your account of Poetics of Place and Narratives, Continued connects bodily experience with architectural interpretation and writing (https://www.lhi.is/vidburdur/the-art-of-architecture-sensing-meditating-and-communicating-immaterial-space-and-material-form/). It suggests a way to examine delegation beyond whether a task is technically completed.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. A proposed library threshold study would compare a quiet waiting alcove with an exposed proxy queue, using sections, observations and participants’ descriptions. A staged AI representative would carry limited, revocable instructions, but a person’s own arrival and continued participation would remain possible. Sound, sightlines and felt permission would be part of the evidence.

Would you critique how the sensory and written evidence could support this architectural argument, and suggest a research mentor or doctoral connection suited to that method?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
