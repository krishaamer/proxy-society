## Prepared text

Dear Professor Vilhjálmsson,

Your group’s 2017–2019 Character Territoriality project treats movement as negotiation of human territories and context (https://secom.ru.is/). That gives me a concrete way to examine whether a representative occupies social space differently from its human principal.

I am developing Proxy Society into an architectural PhD question about AI agents or robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates while personal attendance stays possible. A proposed library mock-up would compare a proxy waiting beside the entrance with one beside a visible staffed desk, using human assistance and ordinary booking as controls. I would observe detours, queue formation and encounters when a mandate expires.

Could we discuss whether socio-territorial modelling would make this comparison stronger, and where observation should correct the simulation? I would also value your view on a computing doctoral home or methods co-supervision alongside architecture.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
