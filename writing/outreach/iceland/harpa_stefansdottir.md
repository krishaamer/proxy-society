## Prepared text

Dear Professor Stefánsdóttir,

Your liveable-densification project connects resident expectations with neighbourhood wellbeing and travel behaviour (https://www.lbhi.is/rannsoknir/rannsoknarverkefni/verkefni/lifvaen-thetting-byggdar-a-hoefudborgarsvaedinu). I would like to apply that attention to everyday journeys at a smaller architectural scale.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. A proposed neighbourhood library study would compare a staffed arrival desk beside the direct walking route with one behind an automated threshold. Observations would record detours, waiting and encounters, alongside ordinary booking and human assistance, while preserving personal attendance.

Could you critique which liveability and mobility measures would capture costs shifted to residents, and advise whether this could fit the Planning Ph.D. and its supervision process?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
