## Prepared text

Dear Professor Þórisson,

Your fall 2026 Empirical Reasoning AI Systems course links hypothesis testing, cumulative learning and autonomy (https://kennsluskra.ru.is/default.aspx?Course=T-713-MERS&CourseInst=40819&Lang=1&Page=Course&print=true). I am interested in how those methods expose the boundary of delegated action.

Proxy Society asks how architecture changes when an AI agent or robot can represent an absent person under a limited, revocable mandate, with the person still free to attend. I propose a civic-room reception mock-up where an agent encounters an unexpected room closure. Comparing a visible human handoff station with a screened service desk, and ordinary booking with staged agent behaviour, would test where uncertainty becomes legible and when the agent stops.

Would this make a useful small empirical-reasoning test, and what failure cases should separate adaptation from acting outside the mandate? I would appreciate a methods conversation and advice on a compatible doctoral route.
