Dear Marcos Zotes,

BASALT’s Treatment Core page lists you on the design team and organizes connected building bars through circulation spines (https://www.basalt.is/projects/treatment-core). The separation of public movement and operational connections raises a specific question for my research.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would compare a non-clinical civic reception mock-up with a proxy stopping point on the main spine against one beside a staffed junction. The agent would carry a limited, revocable mandate for an absent person; expiry or confusion would trigger human handoff. Personal attendance would remain possible, and observations would track obstruction, comprehension and staff work.

Could you critique the circulation drawings and stopping-point criteria, or refer me to someone studying public arrival? The proposed pilot concerns civic reception, with no clinical decisions or hospital-site access assumed.
