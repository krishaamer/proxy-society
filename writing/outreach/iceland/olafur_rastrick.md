## Prepared text

Dear Professor Rastrick,

Hauntɘd combines accompanied walks, discussions in place and camera-glasses recordings followed by interviews (https://english.hi.is/research/new-study-investigates-emotional-bond-people-form-places-downtown-reykjavik). These methods seem useful for testing what is lost when someone delegates an encounter with a building.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. A proposed library arrival comparison would move a staffed handoff point between a familiar entrance and an isolated proxy lobby. Consenting participants could describe each route during walks and later interviews, comparing personal attendance with delegation without treating recorded views as equivalent to embodied experience.

Could you critique this use of walking and video-elicitation methods, and advise whether the question belongs in a Folkloristics doctorate or an architectural collaboration with another degree home?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
