## Prepared text

Dear Sigurður Einarsson,

Batteríið’s Ásvallalaug account describes a lobby shared by swimming and health programmes, with accessibility central to the design (https://www.arkitekt.is/en/project/asvallalaug). Your current design-manager role and the practice’s universal-design work make this a relevant critique route.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would compare an adult civic-facility mock-up with one shared reception queue against parallel personal-arrival and proxy-handoff spaces. A staged agent would perform a bounded task for an absent person, with readable expiry, cancellation and staff handoff. People would retain personal attendance; observations would measure route length, turning space, waiting and whether assistance becomes harder to find.

Could you review which access criteria should constrain that comparison, or suggest a researcher or operator? I’m seeking architectural feedback before selecting any real facility.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
