Dear Tetsuya Sakuma,

Your [inclusive acoustic-design work](https://arch.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/en/laboratory/sakuma-lab/) treats sound as part of access, including the needs of visually impaired people and people with hyperacusis.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for people. At an AI-assisted civic entrance, audible confirmation might clarify a proxy’s authority while disturbing others or revealing a private task. I would compare sound placement, quiet cues and a staffed handoff alcove, keeping the spatial layout and task controlled.

Could you advise on an acoustic evaluation protocol and how it might complement an architecture planning supervisor?
