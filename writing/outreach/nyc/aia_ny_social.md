## Prepared text

Dear Social Science and Architecture Committee,

The committee’s recent urban-comfort discussion treats comfort as a condition for participation (https://www.aiany.org/committees/social-science-architecture-committee/). I’m looking for a similarly precise conversation about who can use a threshold when a service acts on someone’s behalf.

I’m preparing a PhD proposal for Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. A first case would compare a shared civic-building entrance with a separate proxy pickup bay. Personal arrival and staying remain available; the study would track access, detours, waiting, staff work and how people contest an expired instruction.

Could the committee suggest one architect and one social scientist who could critique this comparison? I would value a relevant public discussion or introduction before choosing a doctoral home.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
