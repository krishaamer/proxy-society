## Prepared text

Dear Professor Smith,

CEAL’s street-camera navigation concept lets blind/low-vision pedestrians opt into assistance (https://studentresearch.engineering.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/content/Real-Time%20Blind%20or%20Low-Vision%20Pedestrian%20Navigation%20Assistance%20with%20Street%20Cameras.pdf). Your emphasis on computers augmenting human abilities helps sharpen my proposal’s evaluation.

I’m preparing a PhD proposal for Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would compare a library entrance offering opt-in wayfinding feedback and direct staff contact with a proxy-first entrance that handles collection for absent patrons. AI authority would be limited and revocable; personal visits remain possible. Measures would include independent arrival, access to conversations and the effort required to refuse delegation.

Could you suggest an experience-based evaluation that tests whether the second layout reduces people’s abilities despite completing tasks? I would value a doctoral research-fit discussion through the appropriate CS route, or a public lab conversation.

Best regards,
