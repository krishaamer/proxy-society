## Prepared text

Dear Claire Weisz,

The Downtown Brooklyn Kiosks combine a compact vendor structure with a bi-fold canopy in Willoughby Plaza and Albee Square (https://www.wxystudio.com/projects/downtown_brooklyn_kiosks/). That small service/public-space junction offers a useful architectural reference.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. For a civic-service kiosk, I would compare a side proxy hatch with an open staffed frontage under a canopy. The absent person’s instruction would visibly expire and remain revocable; direct personal service would remain available. I would measure queues, detours and unplanned conversations at the plaza edge.

Could you critique whether this comparison preserves public space and makes institutional authority understandable? A planning researcher referral would help me develop the project as a doctoral inquiry.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
