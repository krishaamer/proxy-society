Dear Deborah Gans,

Re:Play used workshops, listening, storytelling and filmmaking with NYCHA resident designers to reimagine shared space (https://www.gansandco.com/re-play). Its participant-authored approach is a specific methodological reference, rather than simply a design precedent, for my proposed study.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. At a community-room threshold, I would invite participants to compare a proxy queue with a shared waiting/play edge through models and staged film. The absent person’s instruction would be limited and revocable; anyone could arrive personally. The study would ask who defines convenience and what everyday encounters disappear.

Could you critique how participants should author the comparison and interpret its evidence? I would value a methodological conversation or doctoral-research referral, with any community participation agreed separately.

Best regards,
