## Prepared text

Dear Professor Hiba Bou Akar,

For the War Yet to Come examines how planning produces contested frontiers through anticipated conflict (https://www.arch.columbia.edu/faculty/518-hiba-bou-akar). I want to test how a much smaller civic threshold anticipates and organizes risk.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would compare a community-building reception where proxy requests and walk-ins share a waiting edge with one that separates delegated transactions behind an access gate. Personal participation and revocation remain possible in both. The question is how anticipated misuse changes who waits, who can question a refusal and where conflict becomes visible.

Could you challenge the choice of boundaries and suggest an ethnographic question that would make this a planning study? I would also value guidance on its possible doctoral fit.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
