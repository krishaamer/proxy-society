## Prepared text

Dear Professor Irene Cheng,

The Shape of Utopia treats architectural plans as political and persuasive visual propositions (https://www.upress.umn.edu/9781517907464/the-shape-of-utopia/). That is a precise challenge to the plans I would use to frame delegated presence.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would draw two civic reception plans: a gated proxy collection recess and a shared staffed waiting edge. Both retain direct personal participation and revocation of an absent person’s instruction. Before prototypes, I want to test whether the drawings make convenience look inevitable, obscure who can contest a refusal, or persuade us to accept a new institutional boundary.

Could you suggest a historical plan or critical reading that would unsettle those assumptions, and an appropriate doctoral interlocutor for the resulting question?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
