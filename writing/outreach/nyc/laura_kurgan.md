## Prepared text

Dear Professor Laura Kurgan,

From Waiting Rooms to Resource Hubs credits you as a consultant and combines service processes with spatial redesign (https://c4sr.columbia.edu/sites/c4sr/files/project/files/SIDL_WaitingRooms2011_0.pdf). Its resource-hub premise is a useful challenge to optimizing arrival away.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. The pilot would compare a library proxy pickup hatch with a shared staffed resource edge. Both allow direct visits; users can revoke the instruction and stay. I would map waiting, detours, requests for help and encounters to test whether reduced queue time conceals a loss of access to people or resources.

Could you critique which observations would distinguish an improved transaction from an impoverished public encounter? One relevant mapping or participatory method would help sharpen the proposed doctoral study.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
