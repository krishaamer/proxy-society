## Prepared text

Dear Professor Marta Gutman,

A City for Children and your work on Harlem’s Intermediate School 201 examine ordinary public buildings through inequality and collective life (https://www.ccny.cuny.edu/profiles/marta-gutman). That perspective is central to what the pilot should preserve.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would compare a neighborhood library’s proxy pickup vestibule with a shared staffed reception and family waiting edge. Direct attendance remains possible and the proxy’s instruction can be withdrawn. The study would trace whether faster collection reduces access to a supportive public setting, using plans, adult-user observation and historical comparison.

Could you suggest an ordinary-building precedent that would challenge this argument, and advise whether an EES spatial-research or historical doctoral framing is more defensible?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
