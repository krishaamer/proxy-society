## Prepared text

Dear Professor Ciocarlie,

Your work on human-in-the-loop manipulation and teleoperation treats physical surroundings as part of robot intelligence (https://www.me.columbia.edu/faculty/matei-ciocarlie). I’m interested in how a public counter could make a change of control apparent.

I’m preparing a PhD proposal for Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would compare a recessed library pickup shelf with an open counter where a robot and staff member share a handoff. Acting for an absent patron, the robot would have a revocable instruction to handle one item only. Personal collection remains available. I would stage cancellation during approach and measure human intervention, confusion and recovery.

Could you identify a feasible shared-autonomy control comparison for that spatial study, and whether it belongs in a doctoral research-fit discussion or a methods collaboration? I would begin with a bounded prototype.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
