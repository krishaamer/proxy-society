## Prepared text

Dear Professor Porfiri,

The historical Brooklyn Atlantis project combined robot observation with volunteer interpretation of Gowanus Canal images (https://engineering.nyu.edu/news/maurizio-porfiri-three-years-later-still-brilliant). Its division of remote sensing and human participation suggests an evaluation question for my proposal.

I’m preparing a PhD proposal for Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. At a public community-building edge, I would compare a shared observation table with a separate remote-agent monitoring console. An AI/robot representative could carry out a limited, revocable inspection for an absent participant. Personal attendance remains available. I would measure who contributes observations, returns locally and challenges automated findings.

Could you suggest measures that distinguish improved remote participation from weakened local engagement? I would also value advice on whether an urban-science doctoral track or a methods collaboration fits the architectural question.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
