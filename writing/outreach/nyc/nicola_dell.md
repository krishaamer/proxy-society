## Prepared text

Dear Professor Dell,

Your qualitative study of home-care workers’ understanding of AI foregrounds frontline voices (https://tech.cornell.edu/news/home-care-workers-ai/). It suggests a question for my proposal: who bears responsibility when an absent person delegates a task?

I’m preparing a PhD proposal for Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. At a community-service reception, I would compare a private staffed mandate-check alcove with an exposed AI proxy kiosk. An agent could act for an absent person, yet that person could revoke it, arrive and speak privately. Staff would document extra verification and repair work alongside user choice.

If invited to an admissions discussion, I would ask whether participatory work with frontline staff could establish which tasks should remain human and how to evaluate refusal without penalising people. This remains a research-fit question, with academic eligibility still to establish.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
