## Prepared text

Dear Professor Azenkot,

Your participatory robot-guide study preserves choices among escort, guidance and information modes (https://hcrlab.cs.washington.edu/assets/pdfs/2016/azenkot2016hri.pdf). That distinction helps frame assistance without assuming everyone wants a robot companion.

I’m preparing a PhD proposal for Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would compare an audible/tactile consent point beside library reception with an app-only proxy pickup desk. An AI representative could collect for an absent patron, but personal arrival and human assistance remain available. The study would test who can understand, change or cancel the mandate.

Could you advise how to involve blind and low-vision participants before fixing the spatial design, and distinguish useful assistance from pressure to delegate? Is this a plausible doctoral research-fit discussion?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
