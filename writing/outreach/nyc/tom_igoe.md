## Prepared text

Dear Professor Igoe,

Your physical-computing teaching treats bodily action and people’s motivations as foundations for interaction design (https://itp.nyu.edu/itp/thomas-igoe/). Tangible Interaction Design seems especially relevant to making a delegated instruction inspectable at a public counter.

I’m preparing a PhD proposal for Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would compare a reception counter with a tangible mandate token and expiry indicator against a touchscreen-only proxy station. An AI representative could perform a limited task for an absent visitor, who could cancel it or arrive personally. The prototype would test whether users and staff can notice scope changes without opening an app.

Could you suggest a physical-computing prototype and observation exercise to test that legibility? I would value a methods critique or referral to an appropriate doctoral researcher before proposing any workshop or pilot.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
