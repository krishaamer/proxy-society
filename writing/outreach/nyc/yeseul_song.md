## Prepared text

Dear Yeseul Song,

Invisible Sculptures and Two Subtle Bodies explore sensory experience and participation through physical interaction (https://itp.nyu.edu/itp/yeseul-song/). They suggest ways to test how a proxy’s presence could be perceived without depending on a screen.

I’m preparing a PhD proposal for Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would compare a community-building waiting edge using localised sound and tactile feedback with an edge using visual status screens alone. A staged AI proxy could act for an absent visitor under a revocable instruction; people could still attend personally. The study would ask whether cues invite participation, explain cancellation or intrude on bystanders.

Could you critique a nonvisual workshop exercise that tests those distinctions without prescribing one sensory experience for everyone? I would value methods advice or a doctoral referral, with no assumption of programme access.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
