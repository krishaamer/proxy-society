**Subject:** When a learned community rule conflicts with a resident’s mandate

## Prepared text

Dear Associate Professor Shimokawara,

Your [research on robots learning community-specific rules through conversation](https://cs.sd.tmu.ac.jp/en/staffs/staff_shimokawaraeri.html) addresses a problem that a general-purpose representative cannot resolve from a floor plan alone.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture PhD proposal on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. In a community-room entrance mock-up, a robot would learn a rule such as “ask the caretaker before moving this chair,” while its user authorizes only a booking enquiry. I would compare a clearly marked handoff alcove with a shared waiting area, then test what happens when local rules conflict with instructions or the person arrives and withdraws them. Personal participation would remain available.

Could we discuss how to evaluate rule negotiation without treating inferred behaviour as consent, and whether this fits the Human Informatics doctoral supervision your 2027 guide lists?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
