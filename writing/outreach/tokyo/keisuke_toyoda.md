## Prepared text

Dear Professor Toyoda,

Your laboratory’s Common Ground research explicitly considers spaces shared by humans and non-human agents, alongside spatial AI: https://arch.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/en/laboratory/intelligence-12263/. That raises a question I want to make architectural: how does a person see what an agent is allowed to do?

I’m developing Architecture of Delegated Presence, a PhD proposal in Proxy Society. One case is a library checkpoint where an AI/robot returns a book for an absent person under limited, revocable instructions. I would compare a counter and a side handoff bay, showing task scope and expiry while preserving direct access and a staffed cancellation route.

Could you advise whether authority and revocation should be represented within Common Ground, and which failure scenario would best test that representation? I would also value guidance on a future doctoral route and supervision continuity beyond the term noted in the current guide.

Best regards,
