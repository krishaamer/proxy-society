## Prepared text

Dear Professor Narumi,

Your [Unlimited Corridor research on perceived walking direction and physical geometry](https://www.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/en/topics/tp2022-09-16-068) highlights a distinction I need to measure when evaluating access.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture PhD proposal on AI agents and robots representing absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would compare an ordinary library threshold with a mixed-reality version that displays where a representative’s permission ends. Participants could cancel the instruction and continue personally. The study would separate perceived distance, route choice and comprehension of authority from the actual geometry, so an apparently convenient representation does not obscure a detour or human handoff.

Could we discuss how to measure these perceptual effects and define an interpretable, reversible cue suitable for an architectural doctoral experiment?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
