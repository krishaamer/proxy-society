## Prepared text

Dear Professor Ogata,

Your lab’s [demonstrations of approaching, opening and passing through doors](https://ogata-lab.jp/about-ogata-lab.html) make the boundary between a learned action and permission especially concrete.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture PhD proposal on robots and AI agents acting for absent people under bounded, revocable instructions. At a community-building doorway, I would compare a robot told to retrieve a document with one told only to request it from staff. The layouts would provide different stopping and handoff areas. Tests would introduce a withdrawn mandate before the robot crosses the threshold, then assess interruption, staff workload and the person’s ability to attend and remain personally.

Could we discuss whether the behavioural boundary is technically testable, and whether your advertised doctoral route could accommodate an architectural research question with a design collaborator?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
