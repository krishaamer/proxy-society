## Prepared text

Dear Professor Otsuki,

Your laboratory treats residential-area management as a question of how communities control their own spaces, and studies gradual environmental transitions: https://arch.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/en/laboratory/otsuki-lab/. Those two concerns shape a case I’m developing for Architecture of Delegated Presence.

The PhD proposal asks how AI/robots could act for absent people with limited, revocable authority. In a residential common-room entrance, a proxy might return shared equipment or request a reservation. I would compare a shared threshold with a separate handoff niche, keeping resident rules visible and cancellation with a human steward straightforward, while protecting ordinary visits and existing social routines.

Could you advise how to study whether this arrangement strengthens residents’ control or quietly changes familiar use? I would value a critique of the governance and observation method, as well as guidance on a future doctoral or research-student route.
