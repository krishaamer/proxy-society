## Prepared text

Dear Yui Tezuka,

Ring Around a Tree layers classrooms and outdoor play, with some passages accessible only to children (https://tezuka-arch.com/english/works/education/ring-around-a-tree/). It raises a useful question for my project: whose body sets the spatial rules?

For Architecture of Delegated Presence, I am exploring AI agents or robots acting for absent people through limited, revocable instructions, while retaining personal attendance. In an adult library entrance prototype, a representative could wait for a collection handoff; a person arriving themselves would always have a clear human-help route. I would compare the same task with staff assistance and ordinary booking, varying the waiting point and visible exit.

Would your office consider a brief design critique of those threshold sections, especially how bodily scale and furniture can preserve choice rather than make assistance compulsory? This is an exploratory research proposal; no pilot site is secured.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
