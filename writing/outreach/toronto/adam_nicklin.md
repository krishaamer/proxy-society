## Prepared text

Dear Adam Nicklin,

The Bentway uses the expressway’s bents as civic rooms within a continuous public ground (https://publicwork.ca/projects/the-bentway?category=all). The distributed rooms and movement paths, developed by PUBLIC WORK with the credited collaborators, make a strong reference for examining delegated activity in shared space.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. In a public-space mock-up, I would compare a robot handoff bay along a room’s edge with a staffed point on the through-route. The representative could collect one authorized item, with expiry and revocation. I would track pedestrian detours, waiting and spontaneous encounters, preserving personal arrival and the ability to stay without using the service.

Could you critique whether the edge/route comparison captures the public-space consequences? A referral to a researcher who evaluates everyday occupation would help shape my PhD proposal.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
