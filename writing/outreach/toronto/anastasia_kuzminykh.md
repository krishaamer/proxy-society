**Subject:** Can a visible counter mandate clarify a conversational agent’s authority?

## Prepared text

Dear Professor Kuzminykh,

COoKIE’s conversational information-architecture research asks how users understand information locations and paths when visual cues disappear (https://www.cookie.group/). I would like to test what happens when that conversation also authorizes an action at a physical service counter.

I’m preparing a PhD proposal for Proxy Society, exploring how AI agents and robots can act for absent people through limited, understandable, revocable mandates. In a library mockup, I would compare a voice-only pickup instruction with one displayed at the counter as a visible mandate: task, scope and expiry. The patron could revoke it and speak to staff; personal arrival stays available. I would observe misunderstanding and repeated confirmation.

Could you suggest a test of whether the physical display improves understanding without creating unwarranted trust? If this aligns with your work, I would value a discussion of Information PhD fit and current supervisory capacity.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
