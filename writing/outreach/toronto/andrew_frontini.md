## Prepared text

Dear Andrew Frontini,

Albion Library’s courtyards separate age-group spaces while its technology area keeps adults connected to children (https://perkinswill.com/project/albion-library/). The consultation that kept the old branch open is also a useful precedent for evaluating who bears the cost of an intervention.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. In a library mock-up, I would compare a robot collection bay at the entrance with a staffed handoff beside the shared resource area. Plans and observations would test queue obstruction, parental sightlines and whether people understand who can cancel the task, while preserving personal arrival and time spent inside.

Could you critique which plan comparison and community-feedback questions would make this a credible PhD proposal? I would welcome a focused discussion with you or an Albion team colleague.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
