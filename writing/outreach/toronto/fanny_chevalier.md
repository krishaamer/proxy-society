## Prepared text

Dear Professor Chevalier,

Your resource-allocation study separates presentation format from group-versus-individual framing (https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06688). I would like to use that distinction to examine what people understand when an agent reserves shared space on someone else’s behalf.

I’m preparing a PhD proposal for Proxy Society, exploring how AI agents and robots can act for absent people through limited, understandable, revocable mandates. At a community-room booking counter, I would compare an individual-check alcove with a display at the shared waiting edge. Each would show the same one-slot agent reservation, with visible expiry and revocation; in-person booking stays available. Recorded mockups would test comprehension under person-level and group-level framing, then vary placement while holding the display format fixed.

Could you help identify a visualization comparison that tests understanding without treating faster allocation as fairness? I would also value advice on whether the methods contribution belongs in CS or an architecture doctorate.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
