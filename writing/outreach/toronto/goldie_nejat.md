**Subject:** Does a service robot’s position imply authority?

## Prepared text

Dear Professor Nejat,

Your lab’s study of formal and real authority in persuasive robot interaction (https://asblab.mie.utoronto.ca/) raises a useful question for my proposal: can a service robot’s position make a limited request seem compulsory?

I’m preparing a PhD proposal for Proxy Society, exploring how AI agents and robots can act for absent people through limited, understandable, revocable mandates. At a library pickup threshold, I would compare a robot beside the public queue with one in a separate waiting pocket. Its authority would expire after one collection; patrons could revoke it and ask staff. Personal arrival remains available. I would observe hesitation, refusal and detours.

Could you critique how to test perceived authority alongside this spatial change? I would also value your view on whether the research contribution could fit an MIE doctorate or an architecture-led collaboration.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
