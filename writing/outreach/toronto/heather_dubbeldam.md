## Prepared text

Dear Heather Dubbeldam,

Dubbeldam’s Park(ing) Day work turned four parking spaces into a lasting community parkette; the Toronto programme also included an open-air library (https://dubbeldam.ca/projects/parking-day/). That progression from temporary occupation to public feedback offers a useful research method.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. For a temporary library threshold, I would compare a separate robot collection queue with a staffed handoff integrated into the seating edge. An absent person’s limited instruction could expire or be revoked, while personal arrival and lingering remain possible. Plans and short observations would track who gets displaced and whether the representative’s authority is understandable.

Could the team route this to you for advice on a small spatial prototype and its feedback questions? I’m shaping a PhD proposal and would begin with a design discussion.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
