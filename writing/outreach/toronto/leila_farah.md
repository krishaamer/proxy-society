## Prepared text

Dear Professor Leila Marie Farah,

Mobs & Microbes links market halls, civic order and public health in your bibliography (https://www.torontomu.ca/architectural-science/people/faculty/leila-farah/). It makes the social consequences of a seemingly simple collection task a particularly concrete question.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. A market-hall mockup would compare delegated food collection at a perimeter hatch with a shared staffed edge. Direct arrival, browsing and staying remain available; mandates expire and can be revoked. Observations and participant-drawn routes would test whether shorter queues reduce informal assistance or contact with vendors.

Could you suggest a participatory method that captures those losses, and advise whether the market-hall case could make a suitable architectural design PhD question?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
