## Prepared text

Dear Marianne McKenna,

Massey Hall’s renewal separates equipment loading from the old front-door route while adding accessible passerelles (https://www.kpmb.com/project/massey-hall-renovation-and-expansion/). I’m interested in that relationship between operational efficiency and the audience’s physical experience, developed with the heritage and theatre consultants.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. In a cultural-venue model, I would compare a proxy collection point alongside reception with a separate service bay. A staged AI/robot representative would handle a narrowly authorized transaction, with expiry, revocation and staff escalation. Personal attendance, shared circulation and time spent in the venue would remain available; plans would reveal queues and missed encounters.

Could you critique where the delegation boundary belongs without reducing the arrival experience to a transaction? Guidance on an architectural comparison for a PhD proposal would be valuable.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
