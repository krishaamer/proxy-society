## Prepared text

Dear Pat Hanson,

Love Park Pavilion’s three arched sides surround a circular enclosure for a café kiosk and washroom (https://www.gh3.ca/work/love-park-pavilion). I’m interested in the pavilion design credited to your team, alongside Claude Cormier + Associés’ park landscape.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. For a park-service model, I would compare a robot handoff beside the enclosure with one at an arched passage. Its mandate would be limited, visibly expiring and revocable. Plans and staged observations would test crossing paths, waiting and staff escalation, while people retain the ability to arrive directly, sit and use the park without delegating.

Could you critique where a service interface can sit without turning a porous pavilion into a checkpoint? Advice on the spatial evidence needed for a PhD proposal would be valuable.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
