## Prepared text

Dear Professor Paul Hess,

Your department profile puts publicness and equity at the centre of public-space design (https://www.geography.utoronto.ca/people/directories/all-faculty/paul-hess). That is a useful corrective to evaluating a proxy solely by how quickly it completes a task.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would compare a robot collection recess beside a civic entrance with a shared staffed edge. The same mandate permits collection, expiry and revocation; direct arrival and staying remain available. Route traces and intercept questions would examine who detours, waits, asks for help or loses a place to pause.

Could you help identify an equity measure beyond queue speed, and advise whether that spatial-access question belongs in Planning doctoral research or an ALD committee?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
