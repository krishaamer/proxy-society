## Prepared text

Dear Philip Beesley,

Astrocyte’s Toronto 2017 installation translated visitors’ movement into light, vibration and sound (https://livingarchitecturesystems.com/project/astrocyte/). That offers a precise reference for making a change in an artificial representative’s authority perceptible through the space itself.

I’m preparing a PhD proposal for Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would compare a civic threshold with a responsive canopy signaling a proxy’s scope and expiry against a static staffed counter. A staged robot could act for an absent person, who could revoke the instruction or arrive personally. The test would separate understanding authorization from simply noticing a responsive environment.

Could you critique which spatial or sensory change would make revocation understandable without becoming intrusive? I would welcome a prototype-method conversation or an appropriate doctoral referral through the Toronto studio.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
