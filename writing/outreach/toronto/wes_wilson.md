Dear Wes Wilson,

The Schwartz Reisman Innovation Centre combines lower-floor public engagement spaces with upper winter gardens (https://teeplearch.com/portfolio/university-of-toronto-schwartz-reisman-innovation-centre/). I’m interested in the threshold work developed with WEISS/MANFREDI, with Teeple serving as architect of record.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. In a shared research-building lobby model, I would compare a separate proxy reception niche with a staffed point beside the exhibition space. A staged robot could deliver one authorized item, with visible expiry and revocation. I would examine circulation, informal encounters and responsibility when an instruction changes, while keeping personal arrival and staying possible.

Could you critique whether this comparison protects the lobby’s public role? A referral to a researcher who evaluates architectural thresholds would help shape the PhD proposal.

Best regards,
