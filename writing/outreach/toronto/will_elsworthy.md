## Prepared text

Dear Will Elsworthy,

Robarts Library’s reading-room renovation accommodates quiet work, digital scholarship and bookable consultation rooms through acoustic and circulation changes (https://www.superkul.ca/projects/robarts-library-4th-floor-reading-room/). Your biography identifies your oversight of the renovation; the boundary between booking and embodied study interests me.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. In a reading-room model, I would compare a proxy assistance point inside the study area with a staffed handoff outside its acoustic edge. A staged AI/robot representative could carry one revocable room-booking instruction. I would test interruption, sound, wayfinding and staff work while protecting personal arrival, quiet staying and the option to cancel delegation.

Could you critique which spatial and acoustic observations would distinguish a useful service from an intrusion? A focused design discussion would help sharpen the PhD proposal.

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
