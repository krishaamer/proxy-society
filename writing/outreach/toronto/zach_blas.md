## Prepared text

Dear Professor Zach Blas,

Your profile describes CULTUS and Facial Weaponization Suite as interventions into computational power (https://www.daniels.utoronto.ca/people/core-faculty/zach-blas). They sharpen the problem of making delegation optional when institutions decide what counts as a person.

I’m developing Proxy Society, an architecture research project on AI agents and robots acting for absent people under limited, revocable mandates. I would stage two civic thresholds: one asks a proxy to present biometric proof; the other permits a limited mandate and a human challenge without biometric enrollment. Both preserve personal arrival and staying. Film and spatial mockups would make refusal, cancellation and hidden classification visible before considering any deployment.

Could you identify where this comparison still reproduces the power it claims to question, and whether critical installation could anchor an ALD doctoral contribution?

Best regards,
Kris Haamer
Proxy Society
https://proxysociety.org/architecture
