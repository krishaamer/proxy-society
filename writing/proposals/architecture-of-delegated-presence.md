## Research question

What happens to architecture when AI agents and robots can act on behalf of people? Proxy Society investigates how buildings and public spaces can support delegated participation while preserving understandable authority, equitable access and meaningful human presence.

## Central proposition

The freedom to be absent, without losing the right to be present.

Architecture has assumed that the person who wants something is also the body moving through the building. Delegated agency breaks that assumption. A proxy can reduce unnecessary travel, waiting or administrative friction, while buildings must continue to support people who arrive in person. Absence and presence are design conditions that must remain compatible.

## Research questions

- How should a building distinguish a human visitor from an authorized artificial representative?

- Which spatial boundaries should an agent be able to cross, and how should that authority be made legible?

- When does delegated participation improve access, and when does it hollow out meaningful human presence?

- How can buildings support absence without making physical presence a privilege?

- Who absorbs the congestion, verification work, surveillance and maintenance created by automated convenience?

- Which spaces should deliberately resist proxy activity in order to preserve care, quiet, ecology, play or intimacy?

## Three architectural lenses

### Architecture of presence

When participation can be delegated, architecture must answer a new question: what is still worth being physically present for? The aim is not to privilege presence, but to make absence possible without degrading the right to arrive, stay and participate in person.

### Architecture for proxies

Entrances, lifts, service corridors, lockers and thresholds can become explicit interfaces between human authority and machine action. Robot infrastructure is treated as a design hypothesis to test, not a predetermined solution.

### Architecture for shared intent

Places already carry collective intentions such as public access, clean water, accessibility, safety and ecological protection. Proxy Society asks how those intentions remain operative when automated systems increasingly negotiate access, movement and resources.

## Proposed civic-building pilot

Start with one threshold in a university library or community centre. A person delegates a bounded task such as reserving a room, collecting an item or communicating an access need. Study where that authority begins, narrows, becomes visible and returns to a human.

In the conventional condition, human intent and human presence arrive together through reception, waiting and shared circulation. Staff infer identity, authority and exceptions from the encounter. In the delegated condition, the building must distinguish who delegated the task, what is permitted, what expires and when a human handoff becomes necessary.

## Pilot sequence

### Arrive

Human, proxy or service actor reaches the building edge.

### Verify

The place checks purpose and authority without requiring excessive surveillance.

### Narrow

Permissions become more specific as the actor moves deeper into shared space.

### Handoff

Objects, information or responsibility transfer at an explicit spatial boundary.

### Return

The system returns authorship to a person when consequence, ambiguity or conflict increases.

## Architectural test

Can the research identify a spatial design decision that changes the outcome? A better notification or interface alone does not establish an architectural contribution. Compare alternative spatial arrangements against ordinary booking, fixed automation and human assistance.

## Research by design

### Observe

Document how people, staff, deliveries, reservations and exceptions already move through a real building.

### Map

Translate authority, waiting, handoff, access and conflict into plans, sections and time-based spatial diagrams.

### Design

Produce alternative threshold and circulation arrangements rather than only changing the software layer.

### Prototype

Build full-scale or clearly staged spatial prototypes that make delegated authority visible and testable.

### Compare

Test AI delegation against ordinary booking, fixed automation and human assistance to isolate what architecture actually changes.

### Evaluate

Measure access, errors, staff intervention, movement conflicts, comprehension, dignity and perceived control.

## Expected outputs

### Spatial typologies

A tested vocabulary for proxy entrances, shared thresholds, transfer zones, human-priority areas and machine circulation.

### Design principles

Evidence-based guidance for when architecture should separate, mix or refuse delegated activity.

### Filmic evidence

Short observational films that capture waiting, misunderstanding, interruption, assistance and informal encounter as spatial phenomena.

### Prototypes

1:1 or situated experiments that let participants experience alternative arrangements before deployment.

### Critical account

A framework for deciding when no architectural intervention is preferable to adding new technical infrastructure.

## Project trajectory

### Green filter

AI helps a person decide what to buy, save and invest. The question begins as interaction design.

### Proxy society

AI stops only advising and begins acting. The problem becomes delegation, authority and social meaning.

### Architecture

Delegated action enters shared buildings, streets and institutions. Authority acquires a physical boundary.
