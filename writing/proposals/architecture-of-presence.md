## Research question

If AI can increasingly attend, negotiate and act for us, what makes physical presence worth choosing, and how should architecture respond?

Proxy action can reorganise thresholds, waiting, assembly, work, learning, care and hospitality. The proposal studies the value, design and social role of physical space under delegated presence.

## Architectural claims

### Delegated presence is spatial

A proxy does not only change communication. It reorganises where people need to go, how long they stay, which encounters still require bodily attendance, and how institutions stage participation.

### Architecture begins where efficiency ends

As machines absorb instrumental tasks, the value of architecture may shift from processing people efficiently toward making co-presence desirable, legible and humane.

### Presence has architectural gradients

Attendance is no longer binary. Buildings will host mixtures of direct presence, represented presence, remote observation, partial participation and strategic absence.

### Humane space is a design criterion

The Aalto lineage matters here as a value system: warmth, tactility, scale, light, social softness, retreat, and embodied comfort become criteria for evaluating architecture under proxy conditions.

## Humane architecture as a value lineage

The Aalto reference anchors warmth, tactility, scale, light, social softness, retreat and embodied comfort as evaluation criteria. It supplies a value system; the proposed dissertation is not a historical study of Aalto.

## Spatial typologies

### Thresholds and reception

Lobbies, service desks, waiting rooms and institutional entrances will increasingly mediate between present people and absent people acting through credentials, devices or agents.

### Rooms of deliberation

Classrooms, studios, meeting rooms and civic forums will need new protocols for mixed participation: who is really present, who is represented, and how authority is spatially staged.

### Domestic-work hybrids

Homes may become the sites from which people dispatch proxies into work, care and administration, raising questions about privacy, friction, background space and selective attention.

### Care and hospitality

The most resistant spaces to delegation may be those where reassurance, touch, witness and hospitality matter most. That resistance is architecturally important.

## Three architectural scenes

### Threshold / reception

A lobby becomes a negotiation between bodily arrival and delegated arrival.

### Room of deliberation

A classroom, studio or civic forum hosts present bodies alongside represented participants.

### Domestic dispatch

The home becomes a place from which a person sends delegated presence into outside systems.

## Methods

### Fieldwork on present-day presence

Observe offices, waiting rooms, schools, homes, service environments and civic settings. Use interviews, film and behavioural mapping to understand what physical presence currently does socially and architecturally.

### Proxy situation mapping

Trace where AI systems already speak, schedule, purchase, negotiate, filter and decide for people, then map which rooms, counters, queues, desks and encounters those actions displace or transform.

### Spatial prototyping

Design and test rooms, thresholds and institutional situations for mixed human and proxy participation. Work through plans, sections, interfaces, furniture, signage, scenarios and 1:1 fragments.

### Comparative evaluation

Compare prototypes through qualitative feedback, behavioural observation and architectural reading, then synthesise principles for humane architecture under conditions of delegated presence.

## Expected outputs

- A conceptual framework for delegated presence as an architectural condition

- A set of architectural typologies and scenarios for mixed human and proxy participation

- Design principles for spaces of chosen presence, where being there still matters

- A hybrid method combining architecture, interaction design, ethnography, filmmaking and spatial prototyping

## Open questions

- If agents can attend, negotiate and act on our behalf, what forms of space become more valuable precisely because they gather bodies rather than process transactions?

- How do delegated presence and active absence alter thresholds, waiting, assembly, hospitality, work, learning and care?

- What architectural qualities support chosen presence: spaces people still want to inhabit because being there offers embodied, social and emotional value that delegation cannot replace?

- How should architecture reveal, stage or constrain the participation of absent people acting through autonomous proxies?

## Related proposition

Architecture can become part of the harness that maintains human intent. Circulation, proxy entrances, permissions and human-priority zones can make the limits of delegated authority physically legible. The current brief treats specific infrastructure as hypotheses to compare with simpler alternatives.
