## Question

AI acts for us now. What is polite? Proxy Society studies the social rules of AI agents acting on behalf of humans. Etiquette opens the question of what can be delegated without changing the social meaning of an action.

Interaction design used to design what happens when a person acts on a computer. Agent interaction design asks what happens when a computer acts for a person.

## Attention asymmetry

An agent can generate polished communication at very low cost while imposing real attention costs on other people. The website proposes a first norm: do not save yourself 30 seconds by costing another human 20 minutes. These durations illustrate an asymmetry; they are not measurements from a study.

## Six principles for delegated presence — draft 0.1

### Meaning before efficiency

Ask whether effort, attention or personal reflection are part of what the action communicates.

### Do not export attention costs

Saving yourself seconds does not justify creating minutes of reading, filtering or emotional labor for someone else.

### Authority needs edges

An agent that can promise, buy, negotiate or commit needs a clear mandate, limits and a path back to the human.

### Disclosure follows consequence

The more another person would behave differently if they knew an agent was involved, the stronger the case for disclosure.

### Preserve contextual plurality

Access to one part of a person's life does not grant authority over every identity, relationship or community they belong to.

### Keep human handoff real

Some actions should not merely require approval. They should return authorship and responsibility to the person.

## Delegation spectrum

Delegate bureaucracy. Be careful delegating relationship. The value and consequence of an action depend on its context: effort, reflection, witness or personal authorship may be part of what it communicates.
