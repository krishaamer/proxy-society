## Proposition

Proxy Society studies how human intent persists when action is delegated. Intent should remain contestable, traceable and human-governed across agents, organizations, cities and architecture. A prompt for one task is only one layer of that intent.

## Layers of intent

### Immediate personal intent

Bring me dinner. Take me home. Find somewhere quiet. A proxy carries a specific human request into action.

### Persistent personal intent

Protect my privacy. Prefer accessible routes. Do not increase my debt. The instruction survives beyond one task.

### Collective human intent

Keep drinking water safe. Make public buildings accessible. Keep parks public. Reduce dangerous air pollution.

### Intergenerational intent

Preserve biodiversity, climate stability, cultural memory and future options even when the beneficiaries are not yet present.

## Existing infrastructure as persistent intent

Water systems, parks, access ramps, protected forests, building codes and public libraries already express intentions that outlast an individual instruction. The examples below are conceptual readings of those institutions.

## Examples

### Water system

We do not want pathogens in our drinking water.

### Public park

Not every valuable piece of land should be privately monetized.

### Access ramp

Physical ability should not determine whether someone may enter.

### Protected forest

Some things should survive even when nobody is standing there defending them.

### Building code

Safety should persist even when no individual remembers every failure mode.

### Public library

Knowledge should remain accessible beyond the purchasing power of one person.

## Society as a harness

### Proxy

How does my agent preserve my intention while acting without my continuous supervision?

### Harness

How does an organization, building or place constrain many proxies according to persistent human intentions?

### Societal loop

How does society observe outcomes, verify them against public goals, adjust rules and keep humans able to intervene?

## A civic loop

A clean-air mandate could become an active loop: establish democratically determined goals, observe pollution and affected people, act through permitted systems, verify whose exposure changes, adjust interventions, and return consequential trade-offs to human decision-making. This is a proposed governance model.

## Conflicting intentions

Society does not have a single intent. A delivery mandate seeking speed can conflict with a playground's safety, quiet and pedestrian access. The local mandate could require a delivery robot to go around or below the playground. The problem includes deciding who has authority to set, contest and change those constraints.

## Proposed constraint layers

### Rights

Hard boundaries: dignity, due process, accessibility, privacy, bodily safety.

### Law

Executable prohibitions and obligations that constrain agents regardless of optimization pressure.

### Collective goals

Clean air, conservation, public safety, sustainability, resilience and public access.

### Local intent

Building rules, neighborhood agreements, institutional missions and community priorities.

### Personal intent

The preferences and mandates of the person who delegated the task.

### Situational overrides

Emergency conditions can temporarily alter normal permissions with explicit accountability.

## Architectural hypotheses

The following possibilities express delegated authority through circulation, transfer and permissions. They require comparison and testing. The [current architecture brief](../proposals/architecture-of-delegated-presence.md) does not assume dedicated robot infrastructure is the preferred intervention.

## Spatial possibilities

### Robot highways

High-speed autonomous circulation can move goods without turning human streets into machine traffic corridors.

### Robot tunnels

Small underground logistics networks can treat delivery as utility infrastructure rather than road traffic.

### Proxy entrances

Buildings can distinguish human arrival, service arrival and delegated arrival, each with different permissions.

### Proxy shafts

Dedicated vertical circulation can move robots and goods without competing for human elevators and corridors.

### Transfer walls

Secure lockers, refrigerators, waste ports and handoff chambers can move objects across a boundary without granting full entry.

### Machine-readable buildings

Doors, rooms, elevators and services can expose state and permissions digitally so a proxy understands where it may act.

### Intent boundaries

Spatial thresholds can encode not only where a proxy may enter, but whose authority it carries, for what purpose and for how long.

### Human-priority zones

Some places can deliberately limit proxy activity to preserve quiet, play, intimacy, ecology or unmediated human presence.

## Places carrying a mandate

### Park

Public access, ecology, play and low disturbance can constrain a delegated delivery task.

### Hospital

Care, accessibility, infection safety and emergency priority can affect permission and circulation; exceptional access should remain accountable.

### Old-growth forest

Biodiversity, continuity and non-interference can support refusing an action.

### Home

Privacy, intimacy and resident authority can distinguish access to a lobby from access to an apartment.

## Collective and intergenerational interests

Persistent agency could help represent ecological and future interests that are easy to neglect during fast commercial transactions. Who represents those interests, how mandates are contested and how political change remains possible are research questions.

## Research questions

- How can a human intention remain legible after it has been decomposed across multiple agents, robots, organizations and physical spaces?

- Which intentions should behave as hard constraints, which as goals, and which as preferences that can be negotiated?

- How should a building know whose authority a proxy carries, what that authority permits and when it expires?

- Can places carry persistent intent, such as protect biodiversity, preserve quiet or guarantee public access?

- How can collective and intergenerational interests remain represented when commercial actors have faster and more powerful proxies?

- What must remain interruptible, reversible and appealable when society increasingly operates through autonomous loops?

- How do we preserve disagreement and democratic change instead of freezing today's values into permanent machine rules?

- When should architecture separate human and machine circulation, and when should they deliberately meet?
